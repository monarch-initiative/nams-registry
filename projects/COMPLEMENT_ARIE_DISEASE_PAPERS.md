# Complement-ARIE disease papers

Discussion: [#37](https://github.com/monarch-initiative/nams-registry/issues/37)
(the dashboard) and [#36](https://github.com/monarch-initiative/nams-registry/issues/36)
(the report).

## Scope

The [Complement-ARIE Landscape Analysis](../resources/complement-arie-landscape-analysis.yaml)
put 19 questions to each of about 2,700 papers about NAMs, and its
[dashboard](../resources/complement-arie-landscape-dashboard.yaml) publishes
the answers. This project covers the 1,572 papers with an answer to
"Disease(s) Examined": "Is a disease examined in this paper? If so what
disease?" 1,392 of them were published in 2023. Why the corpus is mostly from
2023 is explained in
[the publication years report](../docs/reports/complement-arie-landscape-publication-years-2026-11-13.md).

The answers were generated by a language model. They point to a paper; they
are not evidence. Every fact in a NAM record comes from the paper itself.

## How a paper is processed

Papers are taken in batches. The first batch is the 10 papers already in the
review log.

1. **Pick** papers into the review log.
2. **Screen** each one from its abstract (see [Screening](#screening)): keep
   it, or set it aside with a reason.
3. **Open a DisMech issue** for each paper kept. Its draft is built from the
   dashboard's answers and the abstract, then checked: the models and
   mechanisms expected, and the candidate DisMech anchors.
4. **Extract** the models and mechanisms with an OpenScientist deep-research
   run on the issue. A session then checks the report against the paper and
   curates in DisMech: model links in disease entries, changes to modules,
   and issues for mechanisms DisMech has no node for.
5. **Add** the NAM records to `nams/`, and record the outcome in the review
   log.

Steps 3 and 4 run in [DisMech](https://github.com/monarch-initiative/dismech).

## Screening

Screening records what the abstract shows; it sets very few papers aside.
Every kind of method or model a paper describes is kept, goes to DisMech,
and gets a registry record, including:

- models of human biology: in vitro, in silico and in chemico;
- bioanalytical assays, such as an assay that measures a drug in clinical
  samples;
- clinical and epidemiological methods;
- animal models, which DisMech also collects;
- ecotoxicology methods.

The kinds an abstract shows are recorded in the review log's `method_kinds`,
so that, for example, a paper whose abstract names only animal models can be
found.

A paper is set aside only when there is nothing to extract:

- it describes no method or model at all, e.g. an editorial or policy piece;
- it can't be identified from its PMID or DOI.

Whether a paper has a DisMech anchor (a model in it attaches to a mechanism
node in a DisMech disease entry or module) never sets it aside. A paper with
no anchor is still processed, and its DisMech issue records why no link was
made. Screening for both repositories through one filter sets aside papers
the registry should hold, as DisMech found for its *Toxicology* special issue
([dismech#16882](https://github.com/monarch-initiative/dismech/issues/16882)).

Animal models fit the registry's schema already: a record's `model` is a
NAMO `ModelSystem`, and NAMO's `AnimalModel` is one. Clinical,
epidemiological and bioanalytical methods have no NAMO class yet, so their
records are `deferred` until a `schema-change` issue settles how to hold
them. A deferred record is still written and saved (see
[Deferred records](#deferred-records)), so nothing found is lost.

(Decided 2026-11-13.)

A disease answer on the dashboard does not mean the paper models the
disease. In the first sample, the disease was variously modelled (midbrain
organoids for Parkinson disease), only the reason for the work (a QSAR study
of ACE inhibitors for cardiovascular disease), named so broadly ("cancer")
that it fits no single disease term, or the context for a drug assay
(natalizumab in multiple sclerosis). Each paper's abstract is read before it
is screened.

## The review log

This project's [review log](COMPLEMENT_ARIE_DISEASE_PAPERS/review-log.tsv) is
the one log for the project, in this registry and in DisMech. It has a row for
every paper picked: its screening decision, what happened to it in DisMech,
and the registry records it led to. DisMech keeps no project log of its own
for these papers.

For DisMech, the log holds pointers, not details: the paper's issue, the
entries and modules it changed, the gap issues it opened, and its pull
requests. The details stay in the issue and the pull requests, and a pull
request's status is read from GitHub rather than copied into the log.

A paper not in the review log has not been looked at.

For the first round of 10 papers, the working copy of the log is a shared
[review log page](https://claude.ai/artifact/RpqCWncgKjtbL4kDph94Zr), which
the team can update without a pull request. Its "Copy as TSV" button gives
the rows in this file's column order, and `review-log.tsv` is brought up to
date from it at the end of the round.

### Review log columns

| Column | Contents |
|---|---|
| `citation_key` | The dashboard's key for the paper, e.g. `Zheng et al. 2023 (37215566)` |
| `pmid`, `doi`, `title` | From the dashboard download. Some papers keyed by a Web of Science accession have no PMID here but may have one in PubMed |
| `screen` | Empty until screened; then `kept` or `set-aside` |
| `screen_reason` | For `set-aside`, which reason in [Screening](#screening) applies |
| `method_kinds` | The kinds of method or model the abstract shows, separated by `;`: `in-vitro`, `in-silico`, `in-chemico`, `bioanalytical`, `clinical`, `epidemiological`, `animal`, `ecotox` |
| `dismech_issue` | The DisMech issue for a paper kept, e.g. `dismech#17401` |
| `dismech_entries` | The DisMech disease entries and modules the paper changed, by file name and separated by `;`, e.g. `Parkinson_Disease;dopaminergic_neuron_loss`. Empty if it had no DisMech anchor |
| `dismech_gaps` | DisMech issues opened for mechanisms with no node, separated by `;` |
| `dismech_prs` | The DisMech pull requests for the paper, separated by `;` |
| `registry_outcome` | Empty until processed; then one of the values below |
| `nam_ids` | The ids of the records in `nams/` the paper led to, separated by `;` |
| `note` | Anything a later reader needs, e.g. why an outcome is `deferred` |
| `updated_on` | The date of the last change to the row, as `YYYY-MM-DD` |

| Registry outcome | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `added` | The paper's NAMs are now records in `nams/` |
| `duplicate` | The paper's NAM was already a record; its id goes in `nam_ids` |
| `deferred` | Written but not yet in `nams/`, e.g. it needs a term or a schema change; saved in `deferred-records/` (see below), with the reason in `note` |

### Deferred records

A record that can't go into `nams/` yet is saved as
`COMPLEMENT_ARIE_DISEASE_PAPERS/deferred-records/<id>.yaml`. It follows the
`NAMRecord` shape as far as the schema allows, and a comment at the top says
what doesn't fit and which issue will settle it. These files are not
validated. Once the schema can hold a record, it is checked, moved into
`nams/`, and its row's `registry_outcome` becomes `added`.

## How a NAM record names the paper

A record in `nams/` from one of these papers names two sources, each with the
paper's DOI link:

```yaml
source_resources:
- resource: scientific-literature
  source_urls:
  - https://doi.org/10.7150/thno.80271
- resource: complement-arie-landscape-dashboard
  source_urls:
  - https://doi.org/10.7150/thno.80271
  accessed_on: '2026-11-13'
```

`scientific-literature` is where the NAM is described. The dashboard entry
records how the paper was found, so all NAMs found this way can be listed by
its resource id. The DOI in `source_urls` is also what ties a record to its
row in the review log.

Because every paper is processed through DisMech, each record also has
`dismech_status`, with `processed_through_dismech: true`, and
`present_in_dismech` checked against a DisMech commit.
